\begin{abstract}
We cast tactical highway driving (speed control, lane changing, collision avoidance and on-ramp merging) as a
constrained MDP and solve it with \emph{SafeLane}: PPO with an ego-attention policy and a PID-Lagrangian safety
constraint on time-to-collision. In \texttt{highway-env} we built an on-ramp scenario in which the ego must merge,
fixed a neighbour-detection bug on merge roads, and compared 11 agents and 3 baselines over 26 scenarios
(3 seeds, 49{,}250 test episodes). SafeLane had 0\% collisions on the official \texttt{highway-v0} with the highest return,
and reached 97--99\% merge success (PPO: 51\%, gap-accepting rule-based driver: 88\%). At twice the training traffic density
it kept 94\% success (rule-based: 72\%).
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem and RL formulation}
\textbf{Environment.} We use \texttt{highway-env} 1.12.1: a kinematic bicycle model whose traffic follows IDM
(car following) and MOBIL (lane changes). The policy acts at 1\,Hz; physics runs at 5\,Hz in training and 15\,Hz in evaluation.
The scenarios are \emph{Highway} (train: 3 lanes, 20 cars; test: official \texttt{highway-v0}, 4 lanes, 50 cars, 40\,s) and our
\emph{RampMerge}. In RampMerge the ego starts on the on-ramp and must merge within an 80\,m zone before a barrier; success means it
merged and passed $x{=}370$\,m without crashing. Stock \texttt{merge-v1} instead puts the ego on the main road. Its connected-lane
neighbour search links road segments by lane \emph{index} (the ramp is lane 0, then lane 2), so we link them geometrically.
Training traffic is domain-randomised per episode: density in $[0.7,2.0]$ (highway) or $[0.5,1.5]$ (ramp), and a driver
population drawn from \{IDM, randomised, aggressive, cautious\}. Each randomised car samples its own time gap
(0.5--2.5\,s), politeness and comfort acceleration.

\textbf{State $\mathcal S$.} A Kinematics matrix $s_t\in[-1,1]^{V\times F}$: the ego and its $V{-}1$ nearest cars, relative to
the ego and normalised with a fixed range on every road, with empty rows set to zero. MLP agents use $V{=}5$, $F{=}5$ (presence, $x,y,v_x,v_y$); attention agents use
$V{=}15$, $F{=}7$ ($+\cos\psi,\sin\psi$). Only cars within 200\,m are visible, so the problem is a POMDP.
\textbf{Action $\mathcal A$.} Five discrete meta-actions \{lane-left, idle, lane-right, faster, slower\} over target speeds
$\{10,\dots,30\}$\,m/s, tracked by PID controllers. \textbf{Reward $R$.} Highway (default):
$r_t=\mathrm{lmap}_{[0,1]}(-\mathbb 1_{crash}+0.1\,\ell_t/(L{-}1)+0.4\,\tilde v_t)\,\mathbb 1_{road}$, where $\tilde v_t$ maps
$[20,30]$\,m/s to $[0,1]$. Ramp: $r_t=-\mathbb 1_{crash}+0.2\,\tilde v_t+\mathbb 1_{merge}$. A crash terminates the episode, so it also
forfeits all future reward. $\gamma{=}0.9$, an effective horizon of about 10\,s.

\textbf{Constraint.} $\max_\pi J_r(\pi)$ s.t.\ $J_c=\mathbb E\sum_t c_t\le d{=}2$, with
$c_t=\mathbb 1[\mathrm{TTC}_t{<}2\,\mathrm s]+\mathbb 1[h_t{<}1\,\mathrm s]+10\,\mathbb 1_{crash}$ (TTC $=\Delta x/\Delta v$,
headway $h=\Delta x/v$). PPO maximises $(r_t-\lambda c_t)/(1{+}\lambda)$. After every 1{,}024-step rollout,
$\lambda\leftarrow[K_pe_k+K_i\sum e]_+$ with $e_k=\hat J_c-d$ (PID-Lagrangian).

\textbf{Agents.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Value-based:} DQN; our Double+Dueling 3-step DQN (D3QN); QR-DQN.
\item \emph{PPO family}: PPO with an MLP; PPO with our ego-attention extractor (the ego token queries
all cars, 2 heads, empty slots masked, 46.7k parameters versus 146k for the MLP); \mbox{PPO-Attn} with a density curriculum;
\mbox{PPO-Attn} with a \emph{fixed} cost penalty ($\lambda{=}0.1$); MaskablePPO with a TTC action shield (ablation).
\item \emph{Ours:} \textbf{SafeLane} = attention + PPO-Lagrangian, and \textbf{SafeLane-Mixed}, trained on both tasks.
\item \emph{Baselines:} random, constant speed, and rule-based IDM+MOBIL; on the ramp it matches the target lane's speed and merges by critical-gap acceptance (0.8\,s).
\end{itemize}
Budgets are 50k steps for the DQN family and 120k for the PPO family (8 envs), plus a ``long'' round at 400--500k steps.
Every method uses 3 seeds. Total compute was 10.5 CPU-hours on a laptop; no GPU is needed, because the simulator dominates.

\section{Experiments and results}
Each agent is evaluated on 50 episodes per scenario with held-out seeds and deterministic actions. We report means and
95\% hierarchical bootstrap CIs (resampling seeds, then episodes), plus IQM. The 26 scenarios cover:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Traffic:} densities 0.5--2.5 (highway) and 0.5--2.0 (ramp), including values outside the training range; aggressive and cautious drivers.
\item \emph{Perception faults:} sensor noise $\sigma\in\{0.05,0.1,0.2\}$, 30\% vehicle dropout, a 1-step delay.
\item \emph{Transfer:} stock \texttt{merge-v1}.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Highway.} SafeLane had no collisions in 150 \texttt{highway-v0} episodes and the highest return (33.0). It kept
headway below 1\,s only 1\% of the time, against 20--25\% for PPO and PPO-Attn. Attention alone was not enough: PPO-Attn without
the constraint crashed in 18\% of episodes, with large seed variance. The rule-based driver was also crash-free but less efficient (return 30.1).
\textbf{Constraint vs.\ penalty vs.\ shield.} Unconstrained PPO agents drive at about 28\,m/s and crash in 22--49\% of merges.
A well-chosen fixed penalty matches SafeLane on success at normal density (98.0 vs 96.7\%, P(SafeLane$>$penalty) $=0.38$),
but drives more aggressively (cost 3.2 vs 1.2, near-misses 19 vs 7\%) and degrades more at $2\times$ density (87 vs 94\%).
The learned $\lambda$ settled at task- and seed-specific values (0.12--0.47) that no single hand-picked weight matches.
The shield backfires in dense traffic: 43 of MaskablePPO's 45 crashes at $2\times$ density are into the end-of-ramp barrier.
It blocks unsafe merges, but the agent never learns to wait for a gap.

\textbf{Generalisation.} At half density (outside training), MLP agents crash in 67--86\% of episodes by accelerating into a
lone slower car, while SafeLane crashes in 0\%. On the ramp at $2\times$ density, SafeLane keeps 94\% success, against 6\% for PPO, 49\% for PPO-Attn,
45\% with a curriculum and 72\% for the rule-based driver. SafeLane-Mixed solves both tasks with one policy (99\% highway, 100\% ramp).
Specialists fail at the other task (0--67\%).
\textbf{Budget as a dial.} Varying $d$ traces a Pareto front from 100\% success at 15.7\,m/s ($d{=}0.5$) to 27.4\,m/s with 8\%
crashes ($d{=}8$). \textbf{Long training} raised SafeLane to 99.3\% at 24.6\,m/s, but reduced its robustness to sensor noise.
\textbf{Explainability.} About 75\% of the attention goes to the 3 nearest cars. Integrated Gradients show that decisions depend
on the positions of the cars around the target gap.

\section{Discussion and limitations}
A constraint with a learned multiplier gave the most reliable safety across tasks and traffic levels, without tuning a penalty
weight. Attention was essential when traffic was sparser or denser than in training. An internal review found that highway-env
chose the observation scale per episode, and that the traffic speed factor was applied twice; we fixed both and retrained everything.
\textbf{Limitations.}
\begin{itemize}
\item Every agent was trained on perfect observations and is fragile to sensor noise: SafeLane has 29\% highway collisions at $\sigma{=}0.1$.
The rule-based driver reads the true simulator state, so it is unaffected.
\item The constraint holds on average, not per episode, and in very dense highway traffic the rule-based driver crashes less (4 vs 17\% at $2\times$).
\item The simulator is a 2-D world with simulated drivers.
\item We used 3 seeds, and the budget sweep used 1 seed.
\end{itemize}
Future work: noise-randomised training, an MPC or CBF safety filter, real traffic data (highD), and multi-agent merging.
